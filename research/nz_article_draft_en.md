**Subtitle:** For anyone in New Zealand who buys shares through Sharesies, Hatch, Stake or a bank broker

---

The app you buy shares through already does a lot for you. It places the order, holds the shares through a custodian, sends you tax statements, and emails you when its fees or rules change. This site does none of that.

It does one narrower job. You give it the name of a company, and it looks at the business behind the ticker and writes down what it thinks, with a date on it. Later you can come back and see whether it was right.

That fits into the few minutes before you press Buy. The rest of this page goes through three checks you can run here in that time.

### Two things worth knowing first

**You may already own shares.** Most KiwiSaver funds hold shares in companies here and overseas. Buying a share through an app is the same kind of ownership. The difference is that you choose the company yourself.

**Shares aren't covered like bank deposits.** Since 1 July 2025, the Depositor Compensation Scheme protects up to $100,000 per person at each licensed bank or deposit taker if it fails. ✅ RBNZ. It doesn't cover KiwiSaver, managed funds, or shares. ✅ RBNZ. If a share platform closes, your shares are held apart from the platform's own money by a custodian. ✅ FMA. That protects you if the platform fails. It doesn't protect you if the share price falls.

### Check 1 · What kind of business is this?

Search for the company, add it, and open its page. The first thing you see is a letter written in the voice of Warren Buffett. It describes how the company makes money, what protects it from competitors, and what could go wrong. At the end it gives a grade.

`[待生产确认：拿一只 NZX 股票的英文信，摘一句真实原文做示例 —— 候选 FPH.NZ / AIA.NZ / SPK.NZ]`

**What to look for:** whether the letter names a specific risk. A letter that names none hasn't done its job, and that tells you something too.

**What the letter is:** a language model writes it from the company's financial data and recent news. A person doesn't write it. The page shows the date the data was pulled. `[待生产确认：页面上数据日期的位置和措辞]`

### Check 2 · Is today's price high or low for this company?

A price on its own says very little. The useful question is whether the company looks expensive or cheap compared with how the market has priced it over the past five years. The page gives that as a percentile. For example, "20th percentile" means it has been more expensive than this for most of the last five years.

`[待生产确认：NZX 股票页上这张估值卡的英文原文]`

**Where this goes wrong:** a low price can mean the business is getting worse. That's why the page puts the valuation next to its read on the business trend (getting stronger, holding steady, or weakening). A cheap price on a weakening business is a warning, not a bargain. One-off profits can also make a company look cheaper than it is. Where the site can detect that, it shows the adjusted figure.

### Check 3 · What did this site say before, and was it right?

Each company's page keeps a history of past grades. `[待生产确认：archive 页英文是否完整]` A separate page, **Prediction Accuracy**, tracks how this site's calls have turned out, including a list of recent wrong calls. `[待生产确认：accuracy 页是否覆盖 NZ/美股]`

Most investing content tells you what someone thinks today. Far less of it shows you how the last call turned out. This check is the main reason the site exists.

### One tax note

If you hold overseas shares directly (US shares through Hatch or Stake, for example), the foreign investment fund (FIF) rules apply once their total cost goes over $50,000 at any point in the tax year. ✅ IRD. Most ASX-listed Australian companies are exempt and don't count toward that total. ✅ IRD. A bill now before Parliament would raise the threshold to $100,000 from the 2026–27 tax year. ✅ IRD 2026-05-28, introduced 2026-09-10, not yet law as at [核对日]. Check Inland Revenue for the current figure. This site doesn't do tax.
